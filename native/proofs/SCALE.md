# Native checkpoint capacity and launch limits

The candidate performs O(registered validators + occupied beacon blocks since the last accepted portfolio) work per checkpoint. User reward and loss accounting remains independent of the number of stakers. Every admitted validator stays in the registry, including exited and terminal-zero records. Validator turnover therefore increases the cost of all subsequent checkpoints. The current candidate enforces a maximum of 64 lifetime admissions in the portfolio and prevents new preparations once that cap is full. The earlier funded lifecycle graph predates this cap; its successful history measurement used an empty pool registry.

The successful disposable-network checkpoint covered 1,184 occupied blocks with an empty pool registry. It required 100 mined transactions: one account-proof begin and 99 executor calls. Total gas was 455,302,752. The largest calldata was 106,948 bytes. The final atomic transaction used 3,476,574 gas and committed the portfolio, settled the ledger and activated 42,000 QRL of pending deposits at age 62 against the immutable 64-slot limit. The two earlier attempts exceeded that limit and committed no portfolio snapshot. This successful catch-up establishes a measured local case with a narrow margin.

Ordinary history groups of 12 blocks measured approximately 4.60 million gas and 106,564 calldata bytes. Larger native deposit or withdrawal lists can increase these costs. Three existing four-block calls fit the native 128 KiB signed-transaction cap for the observed blocks; four such calls exceed it. The live driver limits calldata to 120,000 bytes before adding the native signature and transaction envelope. The local block gas limit is 20 million. Reducing the proof transaction gas limit from 8.5 million to 6 million allowed four ordinary groups per block in this run; packing must be measured with actual block contents.

One registered validator witness measured 7,044 calldata bytes and approximately 284,000 to 307,000 gas in actual QRVM execution. A verifier call accepts at most four validators. The following projections use 300,000 gas and 7,044 bytes per validator. They are arithmetic estimates for possible future architectures, not large-registry executions. Counts above 64 are unreachable through admission in the current capped candidate. The gas-only lower bound assumes the entire 20-million-gas block is available and ignores batching overhead.

Native block-flow proofs, account proofs, committee verification, node traffic, signatures, transaction packing and capture time add to these figures. A 10,000-entry registry alone exceeds a 64-slot freshness window even under the ideal gas-only calculation. A 1,000-entry registry needs substantial shared-block capacity and remaining freshness after finality lag. The local reader has a separate 1,024-entry export guard; the candidate contract's tighter 64-entry admission cap governs production-candidate registry growth. Neither reader nor contract bounds replace a load qualification at the supported maximum. No 100,000-validator or 100,000-node test was performed.

The global committee has 128 positions; every accepted certificate needs 86 authenticated signatures. The current verifier verifies every submitted seat's native signature, including repeated public keys occupying different seats. It caches public-key root bindings. A recent actual local update used ten transactions, including eight vote batches, 23,340,634 gas and 718,824 calldata bytes. This global certificate cost does not scale with the pool's own validator count. Certificates and complete economic checkpoints both need enough execution capacity.

A steady interval of the funded lifecycle, execution blocks 1984 inclusive through 2944 exclusive, recorded 731 successful finality transactions using 1,704,088,479 gas and 128 portfolio transactions using 419,682,747 gas. Four other lifecycle transactions used 432,692 gas. The total 2,124,203,918 gas is 11.064% of the fixture's aggregate 960-block, 20-million-gas-per-block capacity. There were 73 certificate completions, with a median nine and mean 13.042 execution blocks between completions; complete portfolios were maintained approximately every 64 slots. These are actual local receipt measurements for the original frozen graph and accelerated configuration, excluding other graphs and network activity. They establish substantial maintenance demand, not a production duty-cycle recommendation or public QRL fee forecast.

Pinned upstream mainnet configuration uses 60-second slots, 128 slots per epoch and eight epochs per sync-committee period. Ordinary finality progresses on epoch boundaries and can lag the head by multiple epochs. The local 64-slot economic freshness value is incompatible with ordinary mainnet finality timing. Freshness and recovery windows are constructor parameters and require separate mainnet review using actual lag, missing slots, certificate construction, competing traffic and economic delay. Copying the local parameters into a public deployment is not supported by this evidence.

Raw signed blocks in this fixture are large because of post-quantum signatures. Rebuilding the first 960-block interval consumed approximately 19.6 seconds of capture and 18.9 seconds of native SSZ export. A compact cache rechecks the native header root, every complete deposit/withdrawal list and its proof branches; replay of 960 cached witnesses took approximately 0.171 seconds. Each new interval still needs timely capture and complete verification. Cache input provides no finality authority, and the deployed contracts verify the complete witnesses again.

A launch requires a stated lifetime registry capacity, a conservative checkpoint duty cycle, measured worst-case validator and block batches, a gas/fee budget, and redundant permissionless submission infrastructure. The candidate's enforced 64-entry cap prevents registry growth beyond that bound; full-capacity, high-traffic execution still needs qualification. Once all 64 lifetime positions have been used, preparations and new validator admissions stop while existing accounting and exits remain available. Longer freshness windows trade liveness margin against economic delay and require separate review.

Retirement needs an explicit accounting design. A terminal-zero observation cannot by itself remove a key permanently: unchanged native rules permit later deposits to the same public key and recipient. A future retirement mechanism must define ownership and fee treatment for every later deposit or withdrawal and preserve complete balance/flow reconciliation. A reviewed accumulator or succinct proof system could reduce verification cost, but it is a separate dependency. The candidate has no retirement path. The public service must disclose its lifetime limit and provide a user-triggered exit and migration policy before that limit is reached, or implement a separately reviewed retirement design. The disposable single-validator lifecycle does not establish full-capacity operating margins.

Pool accounting health is distinct from finality health. The original funded lifecycle graph starts recovery only when the global finality verifier expires. Continued valid finality updates can therefore coexist with an unfinishable portfolio and prevent that recovery condition from occurring. The current candidate adds an immutable recovery timeout tied to the last complete checkpoint actually applied by the pool. Fresh global certificates and partial portfolio work do not renew it. Source and VM tests cover stale portfolio work while finality remains valid. A separate live graph then applied a genuine complete checkpoint at slot 2696 with 10 QRL active principal and a 96-slot timeout. At execution slot 2826, an independent account began recovery after the pool's deadline 2792 while global finality at slot 2776 remained healthy. The depositor recovered exactly 10 QRL at slot 2834, with zero operator fees and zero pool cash remaining. This liquid-principal test used no validator signing key or synthetic state; initial bootstrap trust and the full validator lifecycle remain separate boundaries. Its artifacts and receipts have separate provenance from the original funded graph.
